# Scientific & Mathematical Background: EEG Time-Frequency Analysis

This document provides the theoretical foundations, mathematical formulations, and neurophysiological principles implemented in **NeuroLab**.

---

## 1. The Physics and Neurobiology of Scalp EEG

Electroencephalography records macroscopic electrical field potentials generated by the summation of excitatory and inhibitory post-synaptic potentials (EPSPs and IPSPs) occurring in radially aligned cortical pyramidal neurons. 

### Why Oscillations Emerge
Rhythmic oscillations are generated by resonant feedback loops between the thalamus and cortex (thalamocortical loops) and local recurrent interneuron networks (GABAergic inhibitory interneurons pacing pyramidal firing). These synchronous rhythms coordinate information transfer, sensory gating, and motor execution.

## 2. Limitations of the Classical Fourier Transform

The classical continuous Fourier Transform is defined as:

$$X(f) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t) e^{-j 2\pi f t} dt$$

### The Non-Stationarity Problem
The Fourier transform assumes **stationarity**: the statistical properties and spectral frequency content of the signal are time-invariant over the integration interval. 

EEG is inherently **non-stationary**:
- Sensory stimuli evoke transient changes lasting only a few hundred milliseconds.
- Cognitive motor imagery suppresses mu/beta rhythms within 300ms.
- Opening the eyes blocks occipital alpha within 200ms.

Integrating over the full duration averages away when frequencies appeared, destroying temporal causality.

---

## 3. Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT)

To overcome global integration, Dennis Gabor (1946) introduced the Short-Time Fourier Transform by multiplying the signal with a moving window function $w(t)$:

$$STFT\{x(t)\}(\tau, f) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t) w(t - \tau) e^{-j 2\pi f t} dt$$

The power spectrogram is computed as the squared magnitude:

$$P(\tau, f) = |STFT\{x(t)\}(\tau, f)|^2$$

### The Gabor-Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle
The fundamental theorem of time-frequency analysis states that time resolution $\Delta t$ and frequency resolution $\Delta f$ cannot be made arbitrarily small simultaneously:

$$\Delta t \cdot \Delta f \ge \frac{1}{4\pi}$$

- **Long analysis window $N_{perseg}$:** $\Delta t$ increases (poor temporal resolution), $\Delta f$ decreases (sharp frequency resolution).
- **Short analysis window $N_{perseg}$:** $\Delta t$ decreases (sharp temporal resolution), $\Delta f$ increases (broad, smeared frequency resolution).
- **Limitation of STFT:** The window width is uniform across all frequencies. A 1-second window gives 1 Hz resolution at 2 Hz delta and at 50 Hz gamma alike.

## 4. Continuous Morlet Wavelet Transform

To provide adaptive resolution, Jean Morlet introduced the Morlet wavelet: a complex sinusoidal wave enveloped by a Gaussian window:

$$\psi(t, f) = A \cdot e^{-t^2 / (2 \sigma_t^2)} \cdot e^{j 2\pi f t}$$

Where the standard deviation in time $\sigma_t$ is determined by the chosen number of cycles $m = n_{cycles}$:

$$\sigma_t = \frac{m}{2\pi f}$$

The corresponding frequency standard deviation $\sigma_f$ is:

$$\sigma_f = \frac{1}{2\pi \sigma_t} = \frac{f}{m}$$

### Adaptive Resolution Property
- At **low frequencies** (e.g. 2 Hz Delta): $\sigma_t$ is large $\to$ long time window, sharp frequency resolution ($\Delta f$ small).
- At **high frequencies** (e.g. 40 Hz Gamma): $\sigma_t$ is small $\to$ brief time window, excellent temporal resolution ($\Delta t$ small).

This reflects biological reality: slow neural rhythms require multiple seconds to manifest, whereas high-frequency gamma bursts represent millisecond-scale cognitive coordination.

## 5. Multitaper Spectral Estimation

Proposed by David Thomson (1982), the Multitaper method addresses the high variance of single-window spectral estimates. Instead of one window, it computes spectral estimates using $K$ mutually orthogonal Discrete Prolate Spheroidal Sequences (DPSS, or Slepian sequences) $w_k(t)$:

$$\hat{S}_{MT}(f) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=0}^{K-1} \left| \int x(t) w_k(t) e^{-j 2\pi f t} dt \right|^2$$

The number of optimal orthogonal tapers $K$ is determined by the **Time-Bandwidth Product** $NW$:

$$K = \lfloor 2NW \rfloor - 1$$

Because the tapers are mutually orthogonal, the resulting spectral estimates are statistically independent. Averaging them reduces the variance by a factor of approximately $1/K$, eliminating spurious noise spikes.

## 6. Baseline Normalization Mathematics

Raw EEG spectral power naturally exhibits a $1/f^\alpha$ "pink noise" distribution, where lower frequencies (delta/theta) possess orders of magnitude more absolute power than higher frequencies (beta/gamma).

To detect event-related power changes (ERD/ERS), power is normalized relative to a pre-stimulus baseline interval $[t_{b1}, t_{b2}]$:

$$\mu_{base}(f) = \frac{1}{N_{base}} \sum_{t \in [t_{b1}, t_{b2}]} P(f, t)$$

### Normalization Formulations:
1. **Additive Mean Subtraction:**
   $$P_{mean}(f, t) = P(f, t) - \mu_{base}(f)$$
2. **Relative Power (Ratio):**
   $$P_{ratio}(f, t) = \frac{P(f, t)}{\mu_{base}(f)}$$
3. **Decibel Scale ($dB$):**
   $$P_{dB}(f, t) = 10 \log_{10}\left( \frac{P(f, t)}{\mu_{base}(f)} \right)$$
4. **Percent Change (ERD/ERS %):**
   $$P_{percent}(f, t) = \frac{P(f, t) - \mu_{base}(f)}{\mu_{base}(f)} \times 100$$
5. **Z-Score Normalization:**
   $$P_{z}(f, t) = \frac{P(f, t) - \mu_{base}(f)}{\sigma_{base}(f)}$$

## 7. Neurophysiological Phenomena in BCI

### Occipital Alpha Blocking (Hans Berger, 1929)
When a person's eyes are closed, lack of visual input leads to synchronous 8–13 Hz resonance across primary visual cortex (Brodmann area 17/18, electrodes Oz, O1, O2). When the eyes open, visual input causes desynchronization of cortical pyramidal cells, collapsing alpha power within 200ms.

### Event-Related Desynchronization (ERD) & Synchronization (ERS)
- **ERD:** Decrease in oscillatory power due to cortical activation.
- **ERS:** Increase in oscillatory power due to cortical idling or active inhibition.
- **Sensorimotor Rhythms in Motor Imagery:**
  - Imagining left-hand movement induces **contralateral ERD** over right motor cortex (C4) in mu (8–13 Hz) and beta (13–30 Hz) bands.
  - Imagining right-hand movement induces **contralateral ERD** over left motor cortex (C3).
  - This spatial-spectral contrast is the fundamental mechanism driving motor neuroprosthetics and BCI spellers.
